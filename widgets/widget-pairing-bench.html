Why aren’t all six weights equal?

The average is 450 g. The 400 g cup would need a 50 g saucer to make a 450 g set, and there isn’t one. This pairing has the smallest possible spread for these pieces.

Same pieces. Different partners.

Imagine choosing one completed set at random, with each of the six equally likely. Every cup and every saucer still appears once. Their separate weight distributions stay fixed; changing the partners changes the distribution of their sum.

This unit calls the way two quantities vary together covariance. It helps determine the spread of their sum. The weight spread shown here is the standard deviation of the six possible totals.

How to play

The studio is setting out six cup-and-saucer sets for display. The goal is sets as close in weight as these pieces allow.

Weight shows in the clay: thicker walls, wider rims and heavier feet weigh more. The number on each piece is exact.

Swap saucers by pressing one and then another, or by dragging one onto another. Cups stay in their places, and every piece is used once.

Compare sets to see this arrangement's totals beside the starting tray's, with its weight spread: how far the set weights lie from their average. Undo takes back a swap, and Restore tray puts every saucer back.
