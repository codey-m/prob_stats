Integrating a density

Problem sets ask you to find a normalizing constant, a probability or a mean by integrating a density. This page checks one move: find an antiderivative and evaluate it at the limits, using the fact that a density's total area is 1.

The rules you need

  • To integrate from a to b, find an antiderivative F (its derivative is the integrand) and compute F(b) − F(a).
  • An antiderivative of xn is xn+1/(n + 1) for n ≠ −1. An antiderivative of 1/x is ln x for x > 0.
  • Constants come outside the integral, and the integral of a sum is the sum of the integrals.
  • A density's total area is 1. So a constant c in front satisfies c × (area under the rest) = 1.
